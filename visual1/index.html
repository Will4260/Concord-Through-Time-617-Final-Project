The goal of this project is to show that Concord's legacy extends far beyond its role in American history by highlighting how the town has continued to grow through preservation efforts, recreation, tourism, and community leadership. This project aims to present Concord not only as a historic town, but as a living example of how history and civic identity continue to shape one another over time.

The Night of April 18, 1775

On the night of April 18, 1775, Lt. Colonel Francis Smith led approximately 700 British Regulars out of Boston under cover of darkness, ordered to march on Concord and destroy the colonial militia's hidden stores of arms — a mission meant to crush any rebellion before it could take root. To counter the threat, Paul Revere, William Dawes, and Samuel Prescott rode through the night to rouse the countryside; Revere was seized by a British patrol near Lincoln, but Prescott escaped, leaping his horse over a stone wall and galloping on to warn Concord in time. At dawn on Lexington Green, Captain John Parker's 77 Minutemen stood their ground before the advancing column — a single disputed shot sparked a volley that killed eight colonists — yet by midmorning hundreds of Minutemen from more than a dozen towns had converged on North Bridge, fired back, and forced the Redcoats into a desperate 15-mile fighting retreat to Boston. The American Revolution had begun.
